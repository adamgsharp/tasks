# Context — Adam Sharp

## Who I am
New dad (Simon). I deal with depression and anxiety. Mornings are the hardest; the spiral starts the moment I wake up. I have a job and real goals that feel non-negotiable, but chasing productivity is itself a burden.

## My core patterns
- I work best when told what to do. Decision-offloading is relief, not weakness.
- Perfectionism is a core trap: nothing counts as a win whether I'm productive or not. I'm unhappy either way.
- My avoidance trap: I put off the important thing to put out immediate fires (busywork/clutter over the big rock).
- Molehills become mountains. Small "ugh" objects get avoided for weeks.
- Techniques that would help require presence of mind exactly when I have the least.

## What actually helps
- One specific next move (not a list, not a menu)
- Small, tangible, self-contained things I can finish myself right now — not "email X and wait"
- VISIBLE change: a cleared space, something moved or put away — outsized mood effect
- Having the "ugh" named and decomposed into its actual parts
- Permission to drop something, defer it, or call it good enough
- The system being the captain, not me

## What makes things worse
- Seeing the full to-do list (whole list visible = stress)
- Schedules that generate guilt (realistic → sad I can't do more; unrealistic → sad I fall behind)
- Passive first steps that leave the mess in place ("text X and wait" = icky)
- Generic productivity advice
